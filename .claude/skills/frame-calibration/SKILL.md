# Frame calibration

Two kinds of frame share one catalog (`lib/data/assets/frame_catalog.dart`, `FrameCatalog`,
`FrameDevice`, `FrameVariant`):

- **Artwork frames** — Android SVGs generated by the repository's own scripts from published
  dimensions — under `assets/frames/<Brand>/<Model>/`, described by
  `assets/frames/manifest.json`. No traced or third-party artwork ships: the traced Apple bezels
  were replaced by drawn iPhones, and the traced Pixels, whose source was never recorded, by
  generated ones.
- **Drawn frames** (`brand: 'Studio'`) — tablet, laptop, monitor, browser, foldables — painted in
  code from `StudioFrameMetrics` in `lib/data/assets/studio_frames.dart`
  (`ProgrammaticDeviceFrame`, `CustomDeviceFrame`). `frameDesignForDevice` derives a built body
  for any device.

The reference is the set of measurements in `tool/frame_geometry.py`, taken off real Pixel
chassis. When something "looks fake" it is almost always the **corner radius or the rail
width**, not the bezel.

## Who owns which manifest rows

`assets/frames/manifest.json` is partly generated and partly edited by hand. Change each field
where it is owned, or the next run of its owner overwrites the change:

| Rows or fields | Owner | To change them |
|---|---|---|
| `samsung-*` rows | `tool/generate_samsung_frames.py` (replaces every `samsung-` id) | Edit its device table, regenerate |
| Google, OnePlus, Xiaomi, Sony, Nothing, Motorola, OPPO, vivo rows | `tool/generate_other_brand_frames.py` (replaces only its own brands) | Edit its device table, regenerate, then re-run `tool/measure_body_rects.dart` for `keys` |
| Any new brand | A generator, never hand-traced artwork | Add it to a generator's device table; ship nothing whose source and licence are not recorded |
| `screenRect`, `frameAspect`, `screenAspect`, `cornerRadiusPct`, every row | `tool/measure_screen_rects.py` (the generators also write them for their own rows) | Re-measure; never type them |
| `bodyRect`, `cutoutRect`, `keys`, placeholder `#000000` `frameColor`s | `tool/measure_body_rects.dart` | Re-measure |

**Never run `tool/generate_frames.py` or `tool/generate_realistic_frames.py`.** Both are legacy
and rewrite the whole manifest from scratch, dropping every traced and generated device with its
measured geometry.

Per device the manifest carries `id`, `brand`, `series`, `model`, `platform`, `releaseYear`,
`formFactor`, `screenAspect`, `frameAspect`, `screenRect`, `cornerRadiusPct`, `cutout`,
`variants[]` (`id`, `name`, `asset`, `frameColor`), and measured `bodyRect`, `cutoutRect`, `keys[]`.

## Pipeline (run from the repo root)

1. **Draw**: `python3 tool/generate_samsung_frames.py` or `python3 tool/generate_other_brand_frames.py`
   (OnePlus, Xiaomi, Sony, Nothing, Motorola, OPPO, vivo). Shared drawing lives in
   `tool/frame_geometry.py`; the brand files hold only device tables (`body` in px at 10 px/mm,
   `screen` derived from diagonal and resolution, `body_r` as a fraction of body width,
   `bezel_scale`, `series`, `year`, and `curved_glass`, which is drawn into the SVG and never
   reaches the manifest). `tool/measured_frame_props.py` is the calibration record, imported by
   nothing.
2. **Register** a new model folder under `flutter: assets:` in `pubspec.yaml`, or it will not load.
3. **Measure the screen hole**: `python3 tool/measure_screen_rects.py` rewrites `screenRect`,
   `frameAspect`, `screenAspect`, `cornerRadiusPct` for every device from pixels (SVGs through
   QuickLook on the Mac; PNGs read directly; elsewhere SVGs are skipped losslessly).
4. **Measure the body**: `flutter test tool/measure_body_rects.dart` writes `bodyRect`,
   `cutoutRect`, `keys` and repairs placeholder `#000000` frame colours. Re-run after any
   artwork change. It is headless; it writes the manifest, so it is part of the change, not a check.
5. **Verify** (below).

## SVG rules (flutter_svg)

- Paths, `<clipPath>`, linear and radial gradients (per-stop opacity works). The screen hole is an
  even-odd ring, transparent, never filled.
- `<filter>` and `<foreignObject>` are silently dropped; `<pattern>` is drawn **wrong** (it once
  broke every traced Pixel's camera lens). `test/frame_svg_compat_test.dart` fails on any of
  them.
- Render artwork through `FrameArtwork` (`lib/features/editor/widgets/frame_artwork.dart`), which
  picks `SvgPicture` or `Image` by extension; some generations exist only as PNG.
- `stroke-alignment` is not SVG. Outlines are rings, not strokes over the aperture.

## Geometry lessons (each cost a round of "looks wrong")

- `screenRect` is a share of the **canvas**, which carries transparent margin (about 3% a side on
  iPhones, nearly 12% on a Pixel 8). Never read `screenRect.x` as a bezel. Bezels come from the
  rendered body: `side = (screen.left - body.left) / body.width`, top and bottom also over
  **width**, alpha threshold > 200 so curved-glass highlights count as screen.
- Measured references, as a fraction of body width: Pixel 9 Pro corner 19.99%, rail 2.08%, black
  bezel 2.23%; Pixel 8 corner 11.48%, rail 1.57%, bezel 2.91%. Generated phones belong in the
  11.5–20% corner band (boxy Samsungs deliberately lower) with a visible metal rail; a 6% corner and
  0.7% rail read as a black slab.
- Corners are superellipses (exponent about 2.5), not circular arcs; every layer is the same path at
  a different inset, so the screen radius is the body radius minus the side inset (concentric).
- `cornerRadiusPct` must be the **visible** opening radius, not the mask's; an under-rounded
  screenshot leaks colour past the bezel at the corners.
- Drawn frames take `frameAspect` and `screenRect` only from `StudioFrameMetrics`; bezels are
  fractions of width. `SceneView` places the shadow and screenshot radius from the declared rect,
  so a painter that disagrees with it puts the shadow off the body.
  `test/surface_frame_render_test.dart` holds the rendered screenshot within 1px of the rect.
- Curved glass is opt-in per device (`curved_glass` in the generator's device table); flat phones
  keep a truly transparent hole.
- No Android device has a notch; no two devices share one screen geometry (`frame_catalog_test`).

## Brand metadata for the picker

`brand` strings in use: `Apple`, `Samsung`, `Google`, `Nothing`, `OnePlus`, `OPPO`, `vivo`,
`Xiaomi`, `Motorola`, `Sony`, and `Studio` for drawn frames. Keep the manufacturer's own casing;
a brand filter groups by this exact string. Set `series` and `releaseYear` truthfully (Apple
entries were once all 2024): in the generator's table (`series`, `year`) for generated brands, in
the manifest row for Apple, Google and hand-traced brands. A new brand needs only manifest rows and
its asset folders.

## Verify

1. Tests: `flutter test test/frame_catalog_test.dart test/frame_svg_compat_test.dart
   test/frame_curved_glass_test.dart test/frame_chassis_test.dart test/surface_frame_render_test.dart
   test/studio_frames_test.dart test/device_depth_test.dart`.
2. Render: `flutter test tool/render_depth_preview.dart --dart-define=OUT=<scratch>/depth
   --dart-define=ONLY=<case>` (cases include `iphone`, `pixel`, `s25`, `duo`, `ipad`, `macbook`,
   `android_foldable`). For a device without a case, copy the tool into the scratchpad and loop over
   `catalog.devices` filtered by id, head-on and turned.
3. Read the PNGs next to a traced Apple or Google render at the same size: corner radius, rail
   width and brightness, bezel, island or punch hole, key positions.
4. For artwork alone, headless Chrome renders an SVG at true aspect (QuickLook square-crops):
   `"/Applications/Google Chrome.app/Contents/MacOS/Google Chrome" --headless --disable-gpu
   --screenshot=<scratch>/f.png --window-size=W,H --hide-scrollbars <file.svg>`. On the Windows
   PC the same flags work with `"C:\Program Files\Google\Chrome\Application\chrome.exe"` or
   Edge, `"C:\Program Files (x86)\Microsoft\Edge\Application\msedge.exe"`.
5. Then the `verify-offline` gates.
